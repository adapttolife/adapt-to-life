## 1. The short version

**What changed.** Adapt To Life started as a fund and a directory, and the first campaign was Send 6 to the US Open. In a few months it became something bigger: a wheelchair pickleball crew that plays every week, a network of clubs and organizations, results at real tournaments, a first ambassador who is building the sport in Chicago, our own tournament on the way, and fundraising that never stops. The US Open is now one campaign inside that work, not the reason for it.

**What the numbers say.** Two weekly sessions (Wednesdays at Brick House and Thursdays at ACE starting now). About three posts a week from August to October, nothing since Oct 29. Instagram reached about 334,000 views in 90 days, but only 47 people tapped through to the website in that time. Social finds people; the website is not catching them.

**What the site says today.** The US Open is the spine of the site. It appears on ten pages, including the home page, the fund, the giving page, the partner page and the Brick House page. The weekly program, the Wheelie Big Dills, ACE, the clubs, Monster Smash and the Chicago Classic are almost invisible. The crew's name appears once, deep inside Juan's page. The events page says "Nothing on the calendar right now" even though the crew plays every Thursday. The partner page sells logo placement instead of the value we actually create.

**The new story in one paragraph.**
Adapt To Life expands access to adaptive sports so athletes can find their place. In Chicago, that starts with pickleball. Our crew, the Chicago Wheelie Big Dills, is building a pickleball community across the area that anyone can drop into, one club at a time: drop in sessions with no membership, memberships for players who want to play anytime, and a growing network of clubs and organizations. We fund it all year, through monthly fundraiser tournaments, sponsors and gifts, and we are starting our own tournament, the Chicago Classic. Sending six athletes to the US Open is one of the goals we are raising for.

**The moves that matter most.**
1. Give the program a home: a new page for the Wheelie Big Dills and wheelchair pickleball in Chicago, and put it on the home page.
2. Move the US Open from the spine to a campaign: one goal among several, on top of year round support.
3. Explain the model in plain words: the Hustle and Heart Fund invests two ways, grants to athletes and backing for the people who build a sport. Juan is the first.
4. Rebuild Partners as a two way street: what partners get (audience, content, participation, events, tools, reporting) and honest packages built on real costs.
5. Put six athletes at the center: Juan, Brian, Bob, Robby, Ken and Amanda (Brian holds the sixth spot until RJ is ready), each with a page in their own words that their posts point back to.
6. Make social and the website one system: every post points to one page, every link is tagged, every athlete page shows their posts.
7. Fill the calendar and start keeping score: weekly sessions, past events and a simple impact tracker, so "participation" is something we can prove.

### 3.2 Who we are now

### 3.3 How we invest (the athlete deployment fund, in public words)
Internally, the Hustle and Heart Fund is the athlete deployment fund. Publicly, avoid "deployment". Say what the money does.

> The Hustle and Heart Fund puts money where athletes play. Some of it goes straight to an athlete for equipment, training or travel, through a simple application. Some of it backs the people building a sport where they live, like Juan. When we back an ambassador, one investment reaches a whole crew: the court time, the clinic, the tournament, the next new player.

The two lanes:
1. **Grants.** An athlete applies. We pay a vendor or program directly, or reimburse an approved expense. (Exists today.)
2. **Ambassadors and the programs they build.** We back an ambassador's work (travel, access, materials) and the programs around it (court fees, clinics, events). In return the ambassador brings players, clubs, partners and stories. Each ambassador looks different. (Juan is the first.)

Campaigns sit on top of both lanes. Send 6 to the US Open is a campaign. The Chicago Classic will be another.

Keep the guardrail that is already on the Ambassadors page: being an ambassador never depends on receiving support from the fund, and support never depends on being an ambassador. It protects athletes and keeps the fund clean for a 501(c)(3).

### 3.8 How we talk about relationships: support is not partnership
The principle: Adapt To Life is part of a community, not the owner of it. We support adaptive sports wherever our athletes play, and we only call an organization a partner when both sides have said yes. Said right, this explains every relationship with one idea. Said loosely, it reads as if we are claiming other people's programs.

**The words, and when each one is allowed**

| Word on the site | Means | Who today |
|---|---|---|
| Partner | A two way agreement. Both sides said yes to being listed. Logos only with permission | Brick House. ACE after CeCe's OK (part of the ACE partnership: members volunteer) |
| Supported | Organizations we help with coaching, clinics or volunteers. Kept broad, because these relationships keep growing | WASA (Alec coached and helped start its pickleball program), LWSRA (a clinic coming up) |
| Host | A club where we play or hold events | Brick House, ACE, The Pickle Lodge |
| Our night | A session we run and pay for | Thursdays at ACE |
| A night we join | A program someone else runs, which our athletes play in and our coaches help with. Described by what our people do. The organizer is not named | Wednesdays at Brick House |
| Where we have played | Tournaments and events | APP Vlasic Classic Chicago, Monster Smash |
| Programs people can find | Listings in Adaptive Sports Near Me. Neutral, free, no endorsement | Every program in the directory |

**Three ways we support programs without a partnership**
1. Our people. Our coaches, athletes and volunteers show up. "Pete Petersen volunteers as a coach and helps run a weekly night at Brick House" is true, specific and claims nothing about who runs the program. Partner club members volunteer too.
2. The fund. When an athlete needs help with a program fee or equipment, the Hustle and Heart Fund can pay that program or vendor directly. That support goes through the athlete, so it needs no partnership.
3. The directory. Adaptive Sports Near Me helps people find programs run by many organizations, at no cost.

**Each session, told honestly** (the sessions are a growing network; the honesty rule applies to every one)
- Thursdays at ACE: the crew's own night. We reserve the court and cover court fees, so it is free for every athlete.
- Wednesdays at Brick House: a weekly night our athletes and coaches join. Adapt To Life pays for nothing there; we bring volunteers (Alec and Pete), marketing and social. Pete volunteers as a coach and is vital to running it (Alec helped him build it). New players ask us, and we point them to the right place to sign up.
That difference is the whole fix: every session is described by what we actually do there. Nobody reading the site can mistake Wednesday for something we own, and nobody can mistake Thursday for something we don't. New sessions follow the same rule as they are added.

**Pete is the bridge.** He is on our leadership team, and he volunteers as a coach and helps run the Wednesday night. The site can say both, plainly. It never says he represents another organization, and it never says "every Wednesday".

**Guardrails**
- No GLASA anywhere on the website: pages, alt text, captions, structured data, partner lists, the social page.
- Money copy only describes what we actually pay for. If we do not pay for Wednesdays, no donation or partner copy implies it does.
- Never "on behalf of", "in partnership with" or "official" unless there is an agreement.
- On social, thank hosts plainly when they host. Partner language follows the same rule as the site.

## 8. The outreach script

### 8.1 Principles
- Start from their goals, not our need.
- Show proof before you ask.
- Offer one or two packages that fit them, never the whole menu.
- One clear ask: a short call.
- Follow up within a day with a recap.

### 8.2 First message (text, email or DM)

**Club**
> Hi [name], I'm Alec with Adapt To Life. We're building a pickleball community across the Chicago area, with drop in sessions at clubs like Brick House and ACE. We'd love to host a session at [club]. We bring the players, the volunteers and video for your feed, and our players often become regulars. What would make it a win for you? Could we grab 20 minutes this week?

**Mobility or medical business**
> Hi [name], I'm Alec with Adapt To Life. Every week we have athletes in sport wheelchairs on the court in Lake Forest and Des Plaines, 10 to 15 on a Thursday, and our videos reached about 334,000 views in the last 90 days. That's the community you serve. I'd love to talk about sponsoring a night or an athlete, and bringing your team to our next event. Could we grab 20 minutes?

**Health system or rehab**
> Hi [name], I'm Alec with Adapt To Life. We run free weekly wheelchair pickleball in the Chicago area. It's a place for people to stay active and connected after rehab. I'd love to talk about how your patients could join us, and whether there's a way to support the program together. Could we find 20 minutes?

**Brand**
> Hi [name], I'm Alec with Adapt To Life. Our athletes play wheelchair pickleball every week, travel to tournaments, and our spotlights from Monster Smash drew more than 38,000 views. We're hosting our first Chicago Classic, Wheelchair Pickleball Tournament. I'd love to explore gear and tournament support. Could we talk this week?

**Local business**
> Hi [name], I'm Alec with Adapt To Life. Every Thursday our wheelchair pickleball crew plays in Des Plaines, free for every athlete. $75 covers a whole night, and your business would be named on that night's posts. Would you like to sponsor a Thursday?

**Company team**
> Hi [name], I'm Alec with Adapt To Life. We host Try Adaptive Night, where people play pickleball from sport chairs alongside our athletes. It's a team experience people remember, and it supports athletes directly. Could I tell you more?

### 8.3 The 20 minute call
1. **Thank and set up (1 minute).** Why you reached out, what you'd like to learn.
2. **Their goals (6 minutes).** What would make this a win for you a year from now? Who are you trying to reach? What has worked in other partnerships?
3. **What we do (5 minutes).** The weekly crew, one result, one number, one story.
4. **One or two options (5 minutes).** Matched to what they said.
5. **Next step (3 minutes).** Who decides, what they need from us, when we follow up.

### 8.4 Follow up (within a day)
> Thank you for the time today, [name]. Here's what I heard: [their goal]. Here's what we'd do together: [option]. Next step: [step] by [date]. I'll send a short recap every month so you can see what it's doing.

### 8.5 Common answers
- **"We don't have budget."** Ask for a night, a court, gear or an introduction instead.
- **"What's in it for us?"** Their goal, from the call, matched to content, participation or customers.
- **"We already support another cause."** Offer to co-host a night or an event.
- **"How do we know it works?"** The monthly recap and the numbers we already have.

### 8.6 After yes
- Kickoff: what we deliver and when.
- Monthly recap: attendance, new players, views, events, money raised.
- A 90 day check in, and a year end summary.
