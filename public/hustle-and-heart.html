The Hustle & Heart Fund

More than a check.

The Hustle & Heart Fund is our athlete deployment fund: through ambassadors like Juan Ortiz, it funds and builds with people in the community, so athletes can train, travel and compete. Named for Tim Gruensfelder: all hustle, all heart.

Donate Apply for a grant

Tim's love of the game is part of everything this fund stands for. His story

How the fund works

We fund it, and we build it together.

The Hustle & Heart Fund works through ambassadors: athletes and coaches who grow a sport where they live. Money goes to athletes, and to the programs around them: starting them, coaching them and keeping them running. Each ambassador’s work becomes a lane, and every lane tells its story through its own campaigns.

  1. 01

    Ambassadors buildAthletes and coaches like Juan grow a sport where they live, with clubs, organizations and coaches.

  2. 02

    The fund backs themFunding, plus coaching know-how, club relationships, volunteers and content that helps a program grow.

  3. 03

    Athletes play and competeCourt time, memberships, equipment and travel for the athletes in those programs.

Any athlete can apply for support, ambassador or not. We pay a vendor or program directly, or reimburse an approved expense. How ambassadors work, or apply for a grant.

The first lane

Chicago wheelchair pickleball.

The first lane is the Chicago Wheelie Big Dills. Our first ambassador, Juan Ortiz, is growing wheelchair pickleball across the Chicago area, and the fund backs him, the crew he plays with, and the clubs that make room for them. Its campaigns, in order: every week on court, the road to the US Open, and the Chicago Classic.

The cost of playing

The desire to play is there. The cost can stand in the way.

It’s one thing to try a sport. It’s another to play every week, train every month and travel to compete, and in a wheelchair every step costs more. Here are the real numbers in wheelchair pickleball.

Get equipped

Equipment

$4,000–$6,000for a sport chair, and insurance doesn’t cover it

Sport chairs, protective gear, and repairs. We raise for equipment one athlete at a time, when an athlete needs it.

Build skills

Training

$960can keep one athlete training all year

Coaching, clinics, and practice space. Time to build skills and confidence, at your own level.

Get there

Travel

$3,500about what the US Open costs one athlete

Entry fees, transport, lodging, and meals, for tournaments through the year and the US Open.

Who gets to compete should come down to skill, not money.

"Now I can play pickleball, keep up with my kid, and choose how I move. The chair didn't fix me. It gave me options I didn't have before."
Brian Liss Three straight US Opens
Why it carries his name

Tim Gruensfelder, 1996–2018.

Tim found wheelchair basketball after childhood cancer. He chased loose balls, put in the work, and earned a college scholarship. When cancer returned in 2018, he was 22.

The fund carries his name so more athletes can have the chance to play, practice, and grow in a sport they love. Tim's story
