Our first ambassador

Juan Ortiz

Paralyzed at 14. Hall of Famer in wheelchair softball. Dad of three. Now he is chasing his first US Open in wheelchair pickleball, and building the sport for everyone who comes after him.

  • Wheelchair Softball Hall of Fame
  • University of Illinois basketball
  • 16 hours a week on court

Where it started

14.

“My journey in adaptive sports started at 14, during one of the darkest periods of my life. I was newly disabled, paralyzed by gun violence in Chicago, and struggling to accept being in a wheelchair and attending a new school.

Then I found wheelchair basketball. It completely opened my eyes to a world of pushing past limitations and breaking barriers people set for you.”

Juan, in his own words

Pulled back in

Still the sports dad. A competitor again.

“That first day was hilarious and humbling. None of us knew what we were doing. If we managed to get to a third shot in a rally, everyone congratulated each other.”

“At the end of that first session, the coach asked, ‘Who’s coming back next week?’ It was an immediate, hard yes from everyone.”

“My time as a sideline cheerleader for my kids is completely nonnegotiable. I had to make a hard choice between the sports I’ve played for 20 years and this new game where I’m still grinding to learn as a rookie. I chose pickleball.”

This year he passed on softball nationals to focus on it.

“It’s the first sport I’ve ever found where the wheelchair vanishes, the playing field is dead even, and I can look at absolutely anyone in the world and say, ‘Come meet me on the court.’”

Juan Ortiz

Building the sport

“That sport has to exist first.”

In August, Juan asked the APP Tour’s Vlasic Classic for a wheelchair division, and got one: the APP’s first. Five full teams played it, more than any other amateur bracket there. When lightning stopped play outside, the tournament moved indoors and they finished it.

He has not stopped since. He is asking tournament directors for more wheelchair divisions, talking with the Chicago Park District and local adaptive programs about a Chicago program and tournament, and getting his crew, the Wheelie Big Dills, on court every week. When a tournament has no wheelchair division, he signs up anyway and plays able bodied players.

“In basketball and softball, I was lucky to have huge, established adaptive programs supporting us. Wheelchair pickleball doesn’t have that infrastructure yet. If an adaptive athlete wants to try a sport, that sport has to exist first.”

“Selfishly, I want to build this program so I can compete at the highest level, but the real goal is building it for everyone else too.”

Juan is leading the fundraising for Send 6 to the US Open, and he is the first of our ambassadors.

His seat

Send Juan to his first US Open.

Adapt To Life is raising $21,000 to send six wheelchair pickleball athletes to the US Open in Naples in spring 2027: equipment, training, and travel. Juan is the first of the six.

“Going to Naples brings back that giddy, nervous excitement I felt as a kid heading to my very first tournaments. It’s the ultimate proving ground.”

About $3,500 puts Juan on that court.
