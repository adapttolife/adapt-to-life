What we're building

More than one trip.

Going to the US Open is one goal. The bigger one is an ecosystem for the adaptive sports community to play pickleball in the Chicago area, built with clubs, organizations and coaches.

Trying it is one thing. Playing every week, training every month and competing is another. In a wheelchair, every step costs more, starting with the chair: a sport chair runs $4,000 to $6,000, and insurance doesn’t cover it. Here are the real numbers for each level, and what keeps it going.

  1. 01

    Play anytime

    $5a night, per athlete

    Open play and drop ins at clubs across the area, with no membership and nothing to lock in. At ACE, we cover the drop-in, so every athlete plays free. $75 covers up to 15 for a night.

    Kept going by night sponsors, host clubs and volunteers

  2. 02

    Train to compete

    $960a year, per athlete

    Memberships for athletes who train regularly so they can travel to tournaments. One session a week isn't enough: Juan trains 16 hours a week. A membership can keep one athlete training all year, for under $3 a day.

    Kept going by membership sponsors

    Sponsor an athlete

  3. 03

    Compete

    $3,500per athlete, for the US Open

    Tournaments through the year to build rankings, monthly fundraiser tournaments with club members, the Chicago Classic and the road to the US Open. $3,500 is about what the US Open has cost each athlete the last couple of years.

    Kept going by tournament sponsors, entry fees and donors to the fund

    See the Chicago Classic

It takes skill and a little luck to get to the US Open. It shouldn’t take money.

The US Open picks players by lottery. When an athlete’s name comes up, money shouldn’t be the reason they stay home. We want the best of the best in Naples.

With clubs, organizations and coaches

Places to play, built together.

Through ambassadors like Juan Ortiz, we help clubs, organizations and coaches build and manage adaptive sports programs, so the adaptive sports community has places to play across the area. Some sessions have a sport chair to try or a coach on hand, and some are just open play. We grow each one little by little.

Across the region

We also support adaptive programs across the region: coaching that helped WASA start pickleball in Wisconsin, and work with LWSRA on clinics in the south suburbs. And we partner with Chicago Adaptive Sports, a Chicagoland nonprofit where our first ambassador, Juan Ortiz, serves on the board. Those relationships keep growing.

One goal

Help us fund it.

Every gift goes to the Chicago Wheelie Big Dills, through the Hustle & Heart Fund. One goal for the year ahead, funded in this order:

  1. Every week First$13,500 a year: court time at ACE every week ($3,900) and memberships so ten athletes can train all year ($9,600).
  2. The Road to the US Open$21,000: six athletes, about $3,500 each, roughly what the US Open has cost the last couple of years. Money raised for Send 6 stays on the road. See the road
  3. The Chicago Classic NextOur goal is a tournament with free registration, transportation and hotels for every athlete. It joins the goal once it has a date. See the goal

Equipment sits outside the goal. A sport chair runs $4,000 to $6,000, and insurance doesn’t cover it. Most of the athletes we support have one; when one needs a chair, we’ll raise for that athlete.
