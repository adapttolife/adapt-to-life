How Giving Works

Know what your
gift supports.

Your support has a purpose: helping people take part in adaptive sports. Here is how the fund works and what we are building to sustain it.

How it works

Support that fits
the situation.

We help cover equipment, training, and travel, through direct payments or reimbursement for approved expenses.

Program support helps cover the court time, the insurance, the tank of gas a coach already paid for.

Ask us a question

The people behind the work

Every job here answers to an athlete.

A coach at practice. A mechanic fixing a chair. An engineer building the directory. A fundraiser at the event.

What counts

Safety is not overhead.

Insurance for a clinic. SafeSport certification. Coach training and background checks. These are program costs, not optional extras.

If a program can't meet the safety standards its athletes need, it isn't a program we fund.

Who pays for the rest

Build support that lasts.

Right now Alec covers the overhead himself. We are building revenue so the work can stand on its own.

Adaptive Sports Near Me and our upcoming store, Adapt Body Shop, are part of that plan. Future work will follow the same purpose: serve the community and build lasting support for it. Finding a program in the directory is free.

Right now

Chicago, year round.

We fund a wheelchair pickleball program, including the court they train on. We're raising $21,000 to help six athletes get to the US Open in Naples in spring 2027.

Our first fundraiser sold popcorn and raised $3,004 for the Send 6 campaign.

What we're building next

The rule

When you give to something specific

Campaign gifts support the work named in the appeal. When an appeal includes a fallback to the Hustle & Heart Fund, we make that clear before you give. Gifts already received keep their original terms.

Donations are tax deductible to the extent allowed by law. A purchase is not the same as a charitable gift.

The more we grow, the more we give.
