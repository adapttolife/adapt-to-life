The Promise

100% to the community.
0% to admin.

A promise you can't check is a slogan. Here's what both of those mean.

How it works

We never pay ourselves.
We pay the thing.

An athlete grant goes straight to the vendor. The chair shop, the airline, the coach, the tournament. Program support goes straight to the program's costs. The court time, the insurance, the certification, the tank of gas a coach already paid for.

No dollar stops here and becomes ours. That's the whole reason we can say one hundred percent.

Who we pay

Every job here answers to an athlete.

A coach at practice. A mechanic fixing a chair. An engineer building the directory. A fundraiser at the event. If an athlete wouldn't miss the work, we don't pay for it.

If that ever stops being true, this page changes.

What counts

Safety is not overhead.

Insurance. Renewal fees. Participant waivers. Concussion protocol. SafeSport and background checks. On a spreadsheet they look like administration. On a court they're the difference between a program and a liability.

The test is simple. If a program can't run safely and legally without it, it's a program cost. A junior program without certified coaches isn't a program we'd fund.

Who pays for the rest

Donations go to athletes.
The business pays for the business.

Right now Alec covers the overhead himself. That works, and it depends on one person, which is not a plan.

So the shop, the directory and sponsorships earn their way toward it. As they grow they take over the cost of running this, and donations never have to.

Right now

Chicago, year round.

We fund a wheelchair pickleball program, including the court they train on. We're raising $21,000 to send six of its athletes to the US Open in Naples in spring 2027, about $3,500 each.

Our first fundraiser sold popcorn and raised $3,004. 83 people have given so far.

The rule

When you give to something specific

Some gifts and purchases are pointed at a named campaign. We honor that. If that campaign is already funded, or it can't go ahead, the money moves to the Hustle & Heart Fund. It stays in adaptive sport either way.

Donations are tax-deductible. A purchase isn't a gift.

The more we grow, the more we give.
