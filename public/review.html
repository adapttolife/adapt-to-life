Staging review

One photograph, and the band that was cropping it wrong.

This is live. Pushed to adapttolife.org on 5 September 2026. The tour below is what changed; the links now go to the staging copy of the same build.

The homepage photo band had a bug that was never a photo problem. Its height was set by the height of your browser window, while the picture inside it was scaled by the width — two numbers that move independently, so the framing was whatever window the reader happened to have open. On a wide, short laptop the band squeezed to 3:1 and showed the middle half of the photograph. A face filled it. Every band on the site had it.

Iteration: the homepage band · shipped to production · updated 2026-09-05

  1. Home, a new photograph and no captionThe band is JLA_5950 from the Drive library: a player turning into a forehand against the flat dark wall at ACE. The raw file is a NEF, so the master is Drive’s own 3000×2000 render of it, down to the 1500×1000 the rest of the athlete photos use — 56KB, the lightest band the homepage has carried. “On the court / Different body, same drive” is gone: the picture is already the sentence, and it was the only place that line appeared in the copy.
  2. Hustle & Heart, the same fix, no edit neededBand height is now clamp(360px, 40vw, 740px) — tied to the same dimension that scales the image, so the crop holds at about 60% of the frame from 1280 through 1920 instead of tightening as the window grows. This page’s hand-tuned framing still lands inside the taller band, which is the one that could have broken and did not.
  3. Grant Application, four athletes who now fitThe clearest before-and-after of the height change. Four people at the net is the widest subject on the site, and the old band was cutting the outer two.
  4. Adaptive Sports Near MeChecked, unchanged, shows more of its subject. Listed because the fix was to shared CSS and every band deserves to be looked at, not assumed.
  5. RoadmapSame. Nothing lost a head to the top edge on any of the six.
  6. VolunteerSame, and the sixth band. This page’s band is generated by build-volunteer.mjs rather than hand-written, so it was worth confirming the generated markup reads the shared height like the rest.

Site-wide: The slow zoom on every band goes from 10% to 5%. A 10% drift is a lot of movement to hand to a picture that is already cropped, and half of it still reads as life. The 360px floor is untouched, so phones are exactly as they were — and on a phone the band turns square-ish and crops sideways instead of top-and-bottom, which is why the whole athlete and the whole chair are in frame there and not on desktop.

Worth saying plainly: this athlete fills 86% of the frame height, and the desktop band is 2.5:1. He does not fit in it — not at any band height a laptop can actually show. The crop is therefore a decision, not a default: 35% from the top, which keeps headroom above his cap, the paddle whole, and puts the cut across the wheel below the hub where the green spokes still read. If you want him whole on desktop the band has to stop running edge to edge, which is a different design.

One gap this found in our own tooling: three deploys went out without npm run stamp, so check-site.mjs correctly refused to trust its own results — it was testing a build stamp from an older commit. The stamp exists for exactly this and it worked. Nothing to fix in the code; the fix is to run it every time.
