Staging review

The site stopped promising things we have not done yet.

Two claims were ahead of reality. The fund has made no grants and holds a few hundred dollars, yet every surface an applicant touched read as though funding were routine. And the site said the directory was live in five places while the directory itself said it was not. This walk is mostly words. Read them as someone arriving cold, and tell me where it still oversells or now undersells.

Iteration: claims match reality, in the grant funnel and the directory · updated 2026-07-29

  1. The application pageThree changes. "Where the fund stands today" is now the second thing you read instead of the third, because the honest line was buried under two friendlier paragraphs. The four steps used to run Apply, Review, Approve, Covered, which promises delivery in its own shape no matter what the prose says: step three is now Decision, and it names the outcome that will actually happen most often for a while. And there is a new block near the bottom inviting you to help the fund grow, marked optional and explicitly cut loose from your application.
  2. The email an applicant gets, renderedThis is the piece I most want your eyes on, and it was previously impossible to review because staging cannot send mail. This page runs the real function and shows you exactly what would go out, HTML at the top and the plain-text version underneath. It states the stage, promises only a read and a reply, and never gives a timeline. The last paragraph is the invitation, and the words "no bearing on your application" are a control rather than a courtesy: an applicant must never think fundraising buys consideration. The dollar figure you see is real, read from Givebutter at the moment the page loaded. If that call ever fails the sentence is dropped rather than estimated, because a guessed number in writing to an applicant is worse than no number.
  3. The contact receipt, for comparisonLook at these two side by side. They used to arrive in different typefaces with different sign-offs, along with the signed-waiver receipt, so three automated emails from one address read as two organisations. All three now render through one shell. Nothing else about this message changed.
  4. Roadmap, the correctionUnder "Live today", the card that said "Athletes funded: we've started funding athletes to compete" now says "Funding, so far" and tells the true version: you covered these costs yourself for years, and Adapt To Life exists so it stops depending on one person. Check the wording carries the weight you want. This is your history and I would rather you shape it than accept mine.
  5. The directory, told honestlyadaptivesportsnearme.com currently says "We are not live yet" and /programs returns 404, while this site said the directory was live in five places and offered three "Visit the directory" buttons that delivered people into exactly that. Fixed here: the hero and the closing say it opens soon, both buttons now read "Get notified when it opens", and the same label changed on Karen's page. The nav description changed on all sixteen pages. Check /roadmap too: rather than demoting the whole thing, it now says precisely which part is live, because the site, the program-submission intake and the notify capture all are. Only browsing is gated. If ASNM is closer to launch than I think, say so and I will put it back instead.
  6. Scan destinationThis link is what a QR sticker on an athlete's chair now does. It used to land on the bare donate form. Someone scanning a sticker at a tournament has no idea who we are yet, and the campaign page gives them the goal, the six athletes and the meter before it asks for anything. Signage and hand cards moved too. Nothing was reprinted; the destination was always meant to be editable.

Why the number is in the email and not on the page. The obvious move was to put the live thermometer on the application page. I did not, on purpose. A small figure in front of someone deciding whether to apply reads as "these people cannot help me", and the applications are the dataset the fund needs most right now. The same figure in the receipt, after they have already applied, is the thing that makes the invitation to help land. If you disagree, it is a one line change.

What is parked, and why. The Beehiiv welcome email is the one piece of this I could not touch: it is a dashboard setting with no API behind it, so it needs your login. Same for the publication's postal address, which is still empty and is the only item in this whole audit with actual legal exposure under CAN-SPAM. Peer-to-peer fundraising is also parked deliberately: both Givebutter campaigns are the "collect" type, and participant fundraising only exists on the "fundraise" type, so it is a new campaign with its own URL rather than a toggle, and it needs the crediting policy written down first.

One thing waiting on you. A grant application carries disability detail and financial need, and its internal copy currently goes to hello@, which is the address printed in every page footer and a shared working seat. The code now routes that copy through a setting instead of a hard-coded address, so the moment a restricted grants@ mailbox exists in Workspace, moving it is one line and no code change. Until then it stays on hello@, because a bcc to an address that does not resolve would bounce the applicant's own receipt.
