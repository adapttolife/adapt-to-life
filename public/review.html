Staging review

One tap in, two fields out.

Your call, applied: almost everyone applies for one role, so the multi-select is gone. A card is now a single tap to its role page, and the role page carries its own form: your name, your email, one button. The role rides along in a hidden field. Anything else is behind a shut "optional" toggle. We follow up from the dedicated list, which is what the extra questions were doing badly.

Iteration: fewest clicks to apply · updated 2026-09-02

  1. The board — one tap per cardNo more checkboxes and no "add to my list". The whole card is a link now, so on a phone the entire tile is the tap target rather than a title you have to hit. Time cost still on every card. Tap one.
  2. Grant writer — scroll to the bottomRead as much or as little as you want, then look at the form. Your name, your email, "Apply for this role". That is it. The role is a hidden field so selecting it costs nothing, and the optional note and link sit behind a plus toggle that starts closed.
  3. Equipment technician — press Apply in the sidebarThe at-a-glance panel's button now jumps down the same page instead of bouncing you to a different one. Nobody leaves the posting to apply to it.
  4. "Nothing fit" — the only place free text survivesThe board's own form is now just for the reader who is not on the list. Name, email, and what you would want to do. That is the one case that genuinely needs a text box.
  5. The follow-up email — unchangedStaging only. Still promises a person reads it and you hear back either way, and still asks for no money. Everything the short form does not collect gets collected here, in a reply from a human.

Where the applications land: the dedicated ClickUp Volunteers list, one task per person, named Name — Role with the role in a filterable field, Stage on New, and a "Last contacted" date that is the follow-up rung. That list already existed; the short form just means fewer empty fields on each task. Both shapes were driven against the live list before this went up.

What I removed rather than kept: the multi-select, the "roles you picked" chip tray, and the ?role= deep link that pre-checked a box on a shared form. All three existed to serve browsing-then-picking, and none of them survives your point that people apply for one thing.

Where I was wrong, for the record: I argued against a form per page because twenty-seven copies would drift. That does not apply here, because the pages are generated, so there is exactly one copy of the form markup and one submit handler. I carried a rule about hand-written duplication onto a generated page by reflex, and it cost the applicant clicks.

Still owed before this goes out on LinkedIn: two stray empty fields on the Volunteers list need deleting in the ClickUp UI, and somebody has to own the inbound queue. Every page promises a person answers either way, and that breaks in week one if it is not assigned.
