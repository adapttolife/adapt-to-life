Staging review

A volunteer board, and the nav it forced us to fix.

Two things to look at, and the second one is the one to argue with. There is a new volunteer page: a board of the roles we actually need, each naming roughly what it costs the volunteer. Finding it a home in the nav surfaced that /sponsorship was in no nav on any page, so rather than add a sixth flat item and leave two doors homeless, "Ways to Give" became a Get Involved menu holding all three. The nav got shorter, and one page got demoted to do it.

Iteration: volunteer board + Get Involved hierarchy · updated 2026-09-02

  1. The volunteer board — the whole pageTwenty-seven roles in five lanes. Six are the ones you named; the rest came from reading ATL's own open work in ClickUp, so the board asks for what is actually blocked: the Form 990, the conflict of interest policy the store launch forces, trademark clearance, the insurance application, the sponsorship tracker. Every card names its time cost, which is the sentence that makes a CPA say yes. Tap any card and watch it tick, then scroll to the form and see it listed back to you. The last lane, "No title required", is the one most nonprofits never publish and the one likely to convert hardest.
  2. Home — the new nav, and one line at the closeOpen Get Involved. Three children with descriptions: Ways to Give, Volunteer, Sponsor. Donate is still the button and Grant Application is still featured orange, on purpose, because an athlete must never hunt through a menu. Then scroll to the bottom: under the Donate button there is now one secondary line naming three real roles. Deliberately a text link, not a third button, so it cannot compete with the money ask.
  3. Sponsorship — reachable from the nav for the first timeNothing on this page changed. It is a stop because until today it was in no nav on any page and the home page did not link it either, while carrying tiers up to $50,000. Worth confirming you are happy it now sits under Get Involved rather than somewhere more prominent.
  4. About — "the team is the point" now goes somewhereThe photo band says the team is the point; the closing now adds one line: the team on this page is not finished. It was the best setup on the site for a volunteer ask and it led nowhere.
  5. Roadmap — the hour of your timeThe closing already offered "an introduction, a program tip, or an hour of your time" with no destination for any of it. The last phrase is now a link. Small, but it is the sentence that was writing a cheque the site could not cash.
  6. The receipt email — rendered from the real send pathStaging only, and the highest-stakes copy here. Read what it promises: a person reads every one, and you hear back either way. Read what it does not do, which is ask for money. Someone who just offered their time and gets upsold learns what we actually wanted, so a test now fails if a donate link ever appears in it.

Also changed, but not separate stops: /ways-to-give, where the "not every gift is money" line now points at the board instead of the general contact form; /contact, which offered "Volunteering" in its dropdown with no page behind it and now has a quick link; and the footer, reordered so it agrees with the nav. All 17 pages carry the new menu.

Deliberately unchanged: Donate and Grant Application keep their exact positions and styling. Nothing about the donation flow, Givebutter, the grant application or Turnstile was touched. Volunteer submissions go to their own new ClickUp list rather than into Contacts, because a CPA offering to do the first Form 990 and someone asking where their popcorn is are not the same workflow.

One tradeoff worth your eyes: "Ways to Give" lost its top-level slot to become the first child of Get Involved. It is the most important money page other than /donate, so that is a real cost, accepted because Donate is still a button in the same row and the alternative was a six-item nav with Sponsorship still homeless. If you want it back at the top level, say so and it goes back; the menu then holds Volunteer and Sponsor.

Two things owed outside the site: two stray empty fields on the new ClickUp Volunteers list need deleting in the UI (mine, wrong type, and the API has no delete-field call), and nobody owns the inbound queue yet. The page promises a person answers either way, and that promise breaks in week one if it is not assigned before this goes out on LinkedIn.
