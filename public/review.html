Staging review

Twenty-seven real job descriptions.

Every role on the volunteer board now has its own page in job-posting shape: what you would own, what your first month actually looks like, what you do not need, and what the role is not. Click a title on the board and you land on one. The section to judge this by is "Your first month", because it is the only part that proves somebody thought about the volunteer rather than about the gap.

Iteration: role pages, generated from one data file · updated 2026-09-02

  1. Grant writer — read one all the way downThis is the shape all twenty-seven share. Hero with the time cost and "volunteer, unpaid" stated up front, an at-a-glance panel that answers what it costs you without scrolling, then why the role exists, what you would own, your first month in three numbered steps, what helps, what you do not need, and what the role is not. That last one is where the trust is: "a fundraising quota, we will never put a dollar target on a volunteer."
  2. Equipment technician — then press ApplyA different lane, so you can see the template hold. Press "Apply for this role" and watch where it puts you: back on the board's one form, at the sign-up section, with Equipment technician already added and showing as a chip. There is deliberately no second copy of the form, because twenty-seven forms would be twenty-seven places for it to drift.
  3. CPA or tax preparer — the one that came from your own ClickUpRead "why this role exists". It names the Form 990, the fiscal year call and the fact that missing three returns is how small charities lose their exemption automatically. None of that came from a template. It came from your Compliance and Governance list.
  4. The board — cards do two things nowEach card's title is a link to its page, and there is a separate "Add to my list" control plus "Read the role". The first version wrapped the whole card in a label around the checkbox, which swallows every click inside it, so no title could have been a link. Add two or three and check they still collect at the form.
  5. Board and advisory — the one to argue withThe heaviest ask on the board, and the one where I made a judgement call worth your review. It says we will not sell a seat, that the process should be slow, and that most people who end up in one started by helping with something specific. Tell me if that is more or less than you want to commit to in public.
  6. The receipt email — unchanged, and still asking for nothingStaging only. Read what it promises and what it does not do, which is ask for money. A test fails if a donate link ever appears in it.

How this is built, because it changes how you ask for edits: all twenty-eight pages are generated from one file, data/volunteer-roles.mjs, by npm run volunteer. The culture blocks (how we work, what you get, the volunteer-and-unpaid line) are written once there and rendered identically on every page, so no posting can quietly promise something the others do not. Want a role reworded, renamed, added or cut? That is an edit to one file, not to twenty-seven pages. A share card per role is generated from the same titles, so a post about one role shows that role's name in the preview.

Two calls I made that are yours to overrule: every role says you would work with you, because /about is the only public source for who is here. Naming Karen or a specific board member per role would be stronger and I did not do it, because it commits their time. It is one line per role. Second, no role page states a dollar figure, so the fund's position stays in the receipt email where it is read live and cannot go stale in twenty-seven places.

Deliberately not done: no JobPosting structured data. Google's job markup is for paid employment and expects a salary signal, so emitting it would put unpaid volunteer roles into job-search results as if they were jobs. These read like postings on purpose and must not be indexed as ones. Also unchanged: the donation flow, Givebutter, the grant application and Turnstile.

One thing the checks caught on me: /volunteer had never been in check-site.mjs's page list, so the newest page on the site was not in the live sweep at all. It is now, along with two sampled role pages, derived from the data so a rename cannot make the sample stale. Nineteen pages and forty-nine internal targets pass.
