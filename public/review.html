Iteration: cut the fat · updated 2026-09-02

  1. Grant writer — read the whole thingIt opens with the argument in two sentences and no heading over it, which is how a posting actually reads. Then four sections: the work, your first month, who it suits, not this. "What helps" and "What you do not need" used to be two lists asking one question; the second is now a single line of relief under the first.
  2. Equipment technician — check the shape holdsDifferent lane, same four sections, still under 500 words. The sidebar is one fact and a button now instead of a table that repeated the chips in the hero.
  3. The board — cards are one sentenceEvery card was one or two sentences and is now one. The board is for scanning; the argument belongs on the page. The copy above the first card is 167 words including the hero.
  4. "What you would be joining" — four short termsThese are the terms of joining, so the board owns them and no role page repeats them. They used to appear as eight headed blocks on every one of the twenty-seven.

The rules that came out of this, now in docs/CONTENT.md and enforced by test: a card is one sentence, a role’s argument is two, three work bullets, three first-month steps, two "who it suits" bullets, one line of relief, two "not this" bullets, and the page under 500 words. If a fourth bullet feels necessary, one of the others was not pulling its weight. A test also fails if any page starts narrating its own design again.

Where the sloppiness actually came from: most of it was me explaining the page to the reader. "Nobody starts with the whole job. This is what we would actually ask you to do first, in order." The heading already said that. Same with "Being explicit about the boundary is how we make the rest of this believable" and "Here is the part most organizations leave out of a posting", which was telling rather than showing.

Nothing about the flow changed: one tap from a card, then a name, an email and one button on the role page. The two-field form and the ClickUp destination are untouched.
