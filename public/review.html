Staging review

One card, six sentences.

Same artwork, same style, every link. The only thing that changes is the sentence. Below are all six at the size a phone actually draws them, which is where the old card fell apart: its third line was about four pixels tall.

Iteration: share cards, generated from the site's own tokens · updated 2026-07-25

What changed. The backdrop is your embossed mark, the same on all six, and each page carries its own headline taken word for word from that page. Cards are generated by scripts/make-og.mjs from the same colours and typefaces as the site, so they cannot drift the next time the brand moves.

The pages whose card changed. Open any of them if you want to see the page behind the card.

  1. HomeCarries the default card, which every page without its own falls back to.
  2. Donate"Put an athlete in the game." The link that gets sent the most, so it stopped sharing a generic card.
  3. Send 6 to the US OpenJust the line now. The sport question goes away when no card carries a photograph, though the correction stands: the US Open in Naples is pickleball, and I had inferred water-ski from the photo library instead of reading this page.
  4. PopcornThe card in your screenshot. Paper rather than a photo: the only popcorn imagery we own is Double Good's branded bag, and a partner's logo does not belong on our share card.
  5. Hustle & Heart Fund"Every dollar goes to an athlete," the page's own headline.
  6. Ways to Give"Every road here ends on a court."

One honest limit. A crawler needs an absolute URL for the image, so every page points at adapttolife.org rather than at staging. That means if you paste a staging link into a text thread you will see the OLD card, because the new files only exist here until this ships. The cards above are the real files on this host, at the real size, which is as close as the test gets before you say go.

Two notes on the artwork. Both files you sent carried a Gemini sparkle in the bottom corner, so each is cropped to exclude it, verified by auto-levelling the bottom strip until only leather texture showed. I built the fire version too and would leave it: at card size the sparks and scratched metal fight the serif, and it reads closer to a film poster than to us. The photo layout is still in the generator, one flag away, if you ever want athletes back on a card.

Five checks now run on every deploy. A headline too small to survive the phone-sized downscale, type overlapping the photograph, a card over the 300KB budget that WhatsApp silently refuses to preview, a headline that stops matching its page's headline, or a crop that would enlarge its own source: each one fails the build.
