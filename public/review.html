Staging review

Nothing should look different. That is the test.

This iteration is speed and safety, not design. No page changed its layout, its copy or its behaviour, so the review is the opposite of the usual one: walk the stops and confirm that everything is exactly where you left it, and that the giving form still takes a gift.

Iteration: asset caching, a money-path check, and a page-weight budget · updated 2026-07-25

  1. Donate, on your phoneThe one that matters. Tap an amount, then Continue, and confirm you reach the card step. Stop there, do not complete a gift. This page is now covered by an automated check that reads the amount step the way you just did, so if the form ever fails to load, the build goes red instead of quietly showing a donor an empty box. That check did not exist before this week.
  2. Hustle & Heart FundThe second giving form, same check, same test. Tap through to the amount step and confirm it appears.
  3. HomeLoad it, scroll it, tap the hamburger. Then go to another page and come back. The second visit should feel instant: the stylesheet, both scripts and the photos now stay in your browser instead of being re-checked with the server on every single click. That was four wasted round-trips per page view, on every visit, forever.
  4. Ways to GiveA page with a lot of links and a form. Confirm nothing shifted and the footer signup still submits.
  5. PopcornThe heaviest page we build ourselves. Scroll it top to bottom on your phone and confirm it still runs smooth.

What you cannot see, and what I measured instead. Caching is invisible by design, so it was verified at the wire rather than by eye: every rule applies on staging, and the rules file itself is not served. Scroll was measured on a throttled phone across four pages and holds a locked 60fps with zero dropped frames, which is why nothing about the scroll was touched. Taps on the home page are clean, worst case 76ms.

One thing this iteration does not fix. On /donate, taps can lag up to 577ms in a window roughly ten seconds after the page loads, while Givebutter's embed parses about 9MB of Stripe, Google Maps and its own bundles. That is their code, not ours, but we control when it loads. Gating it until someone actually intends to give is the next change, and it is deliberately not in this one, because it changes behaviour on the giving path and I wanted the check that guards that path landed first.

Production has not been touched. This is staging only. PR #60 is open and waiting on your word before anything ships.
