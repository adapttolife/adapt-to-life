01 · The decision

The homepage header

The header used to be a sentence on an empty black field with the right half doing nothing. It is now nineteen photographs of your athletes, three planes deep, with the words in the corner. There are no buttons in it — feel first, then the ask — because Donate moved into the nav at every width.

Open it on a phone as well as a laptop. They are two different layouts of the same photographs: a wall of twenty-two on desktop, and on mobile four — whole, big, nothing cropped.

The phone went twelve cells → six → four over three rounds, and each cut was the same lesson: twelve cells meant each was 195×130 and the lower eight were dimmed depth frames, so the bottom half read as mush. Then at six, the headline slab still covered the top 41% of the middle row — and measuring the head position in every panel we own, the highest sits at 0.35. No portrait in the library survives that cut, because good portraits put the head in the upper third. So the headline got a band of its own instead of floating over a row. Four photographs, all whole.

It made the phone lighter without anyone optimising anything: it fetches 4 panels and 152KB where desktop fetches 22 and 276KB. The cells it does not show are never requested.

02 · Rolled across the site

Eleven interior pages — now on a height rule

Header heights are now chosen, not inherited. They used to be whatever the copy needed plus fixed padding, which measured 553px to 1072px across the site — a 1.9× spread nobody picked. There are four tiers now: lg 68vh for section doors, md 56vh for standard pages, sm 44vh for forms and legal, and a dedicated tier for Donate sized around the Givebutter panel. And the band fills its header at every tier, so every page measures 0% black — a taller header is now more photograph, not more empty space.

Every one of these got the banner language: the same bottom-aligned columns, the same pitch black, the same orange as a floor bloom — held well back behind each page's own words. It takes variant 2's language, not its layout, because a page header is not five words; it is an eyebrow, a headline and a sub-line, and all of those had to stay readable.

07 · Under the hood

What this cost, and what protects it

  • Switching the homepage header is one command. node scripts/apply-hero.mjs 1 — it reads the same definitions the candidate pages read, so what you approve and what ships are the same bytes.
  • The header is half the weight it started at. Every photograph is sized to what it actually paints on screen: 490KB down to 250KB, with no visible change.
  • The interior band is one image, 19KB, not eight. Shipping it as eight put 450KB on the donate page and the budget check refused it.
  • 472 automated tests pass, including four new ones that assert the homepage is actually carrying the header you chose — I reverted it once by accident and every other check stayed green.
  • The full site check passes on the deployed build: nineteen pages, forty-six links, nav parity, no sideways scroll at any width, no dead links, share cards all resolving.
07b · New

The whole site is Sofia Sans now

ExtraBold for every headline, regular and medium for body copy, and the small uppercase labels hold their role through tracking rather than through a second family. Newsreader and Space Mono are gone. That also settles the serif-or-grotesk split — the voice no longer changes with the screen size.

An 800-weight grotesk is not a 500-weight serif with a different name: it needs real negative tracking at display sizes or it reads loose, and it needs less leading. Every headline size was re-set for it rather than swapped in place.

  • Three CSS variables hold the families, so the next change to the site's voice is three lines, not sixty files.
  • One command applies the webfont to all 57 pages, the volunteer page generator and the share-card template — identical everywhere, because a page that loads a different weight range renders the same headline at a different thickness.
  • The eight share cards were regenerated in the new face, so what gets posted to social matches the site.
