The goal, in people

Six seats. One athlete each.

The number is not a target we picked. It is six athletes multiplied by what it actually costs to get one of them there and back.

    Athletes are selected through the same grant process as everyone else the fund backs. We will name them here once they are confirmed.

    Where the money goes

    What it takes to put one athlete on that court.

    The US Open is in Naples, and it runs the better part of a week. For nearly every athlete we back, that is a flight rather than a drive. Three things have to be true before an athlete can play it.

    • Equipment

      about $450

      A sport chair is only as good as what it rolls on. Tires wear out on tournament surfaces, and a cracked paddle ends a weekend early. Tires run about $200, a competition paddle about $250.

    • Training

      about $500

      Nobody arrives at a national championship untrained. We share a practice facility with six to ten wheelchair athletes every week, all year. This is the part of your gift that keeps working long after the tournament ends.

    • Travel

      about $2,300

      Airfare into Naples in the spring runs around $500. Six nights of lodging is over $1,200. Entry is $150 to register plus $45 to $50 per event, and accessible ground transport is on top of that.

    About $3,500 puts one athlete on that court. Six athletes is $21,000. These are our own numbers from the last trip and the published entry fees, not estimates. They climb every year, so we budget honestly rather than optimistically.

    Wheelchair events run double elimination with a consolation bracket, so an athlete who travels gets more than one match. Nobody flies thirteen hundred miles to play once.
