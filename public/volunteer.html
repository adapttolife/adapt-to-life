Volunteer

Your place on this team.

Adapt To Life is early, and the honest version is that we need people more than we need hours. Below is the real list we work from. Some of it is a career's worth of skill. Some of it is one introduction. All of it ends with an athlete on a court.

Before you scroll

What you would actually be joining.

Small, and saying so.

A recognized 501(c)(3) with a board, a published grant rubric, and a fund that is still young. We would rather you know that now than find out later.

A scope, not a shift.

Every role here has a defined job and a person you work with. If we cannot tell you what done looks like, we have no business asking for your time.

You hear back either way.

A person reads every one of these. If there is no fit right now, we will say that rather than leave you wondering.

Remote is normal.

Most of this list is done from anywhere. The court work is in person, and we will be straight with you about which is which.

Fund the fund

Money, and the people who move it.

The Hustle & Heart Fund is young. Everything in this lane is about making it bigger than one person's checkbook.

Keep it clean

The unglamorous work that protects athletes.

A nonprofit that loses its exemption stops funding anyone. This lane is insurance against that, and it is where professional volunteers are worth the most.

Tell it right

Reach, so the next athlete finds us.

An athlete cannot apply to a fund they have never heard of. Everything here is about being findable.

On the court

The part that looks like sport.

Hands on, in person, usually on a weekend.

No title required

You do not need a profession to change this.

Most people who want to help do not have a job title we listed. They have a relationship, a room, or a reason to organize something.

Not on the list

We wrote down what we know we need.

That is not the same as everything we need. If you have something we did not think to ask for, put it in the box below and tell us plainly. The list grows.

What the hours turn into Somebody found the program. Somebody cleared the paperwork. Somebody paid for the chair. Then she played.
