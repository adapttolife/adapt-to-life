Volunteer

Your place on this team.

Adapt To Life is early, and the honest version is that we need people more than we need hours. Below is the real list we work from. Open any role to see what you would own, what your first month looks like, and roughly what it costs you. Applying takes a name and an email.

See what we need →
Before you scroll

What you would actually be joining.

Async by default

Almost none of this needs a meeting. We write things down so a volunteer with two hours on a Tuesday night can pick up where someone else left off.

A scope, not a shift

You get a defined job and a person to work with. If we cannot tell you what done looks like, we have no business asking for your time.

No busywork

We will not invent tasks to keep you occupied. If there is nothing useful this month, we will say so.

You hear back either way

A person reads every application. If there is no fit right now you will be told that, rather than left wondering.

Fund the fund

Money, and the people who move it.

The Hustle & Heart Fund is young. Everything in this lane is about making it bigger than one person's checkbook.

  • Grant writer Find the foundations that fund adaptive sport, and write the applications. Nobody at Adapt To Life has done this at scale yet. A few hours a monthRead the role →
  • Fundraising lead Build the annual plan. Which campaigns, which asks, in what order, and who we go back to. A few hours a monthRead the role →
  • Sponsorship and partnership lead Bring businesses to the table and keep them there. We have levels published and almost nobody working them. A few hours a monthRead the role →
  • Corporate matching champion Get Adapt To Life into your employer's matching program. The single highest return an hour of volunteering has. One hour, onceRead the role →
  • Planned and major giving advisor Donor advised funds, appreciated stock, bequests. The gifts that need someone who speaks the language. As it comes upRead the role →
Keep it clean

The unglamorous work that protects athletes.

A nonprofit that loses its exemption stops funding anyone. This lane is insurance against that, and it is where professional volunteers are worth the most.

  • CPA or tax preparer Our first Form 990, our fiscal year call, and keeping the exemption airtight. Missing three returns is how small charities quietly disappear. SeasonalRead the role →
  • Bookkeeper Monthly reconciliation alongside our treasurer, so the books are ready before anyone asks to see them. An hour or two a monthRead the role →
  • Nonprofit attorney Bylaws, a conflict of interest policy, and charitable registration in each state as we raise in more of them. As it comes upRead the role →
  • Trademark counsel Clear and file our marks before they go on physical product. Cheapest to fix right now. A one time projectRead the role →
  • Insurance and risk Coverage for clinics, tournaments, volunteers, and the athletes we put on a court. As it comes upRead the role →
  • Grant reviewer Score applications against our published rubric so decisions are fair, consistent, and documented. A few hours per cycleRead the role →
Tell it right

Reach, so the next athlete finds us.

An athlete cannot apply to a fund they have never heard of. Everything here is about being findable.

  • Social media manager Turn our work into posts people stop for, on the platforms where the adaptive community actually is. A few hours a weekRead the role →
  • Writer or editor Athlete stories, the newsletter, and eventually the annual impact report. A few hours a monthRead the role →
  • Photographer or videographer Shoot our clinics and tournaments. Real athletes, ours. Every image on this site came from someone who showed up. A day at a timeRead the role →
  • Graphic designer Sponsor decks, one pagers, event graphics, apparel. Project by projectRead the role →
  • Press and media Get athletes in front of reporters, podcasts, and local news. As it comes upRead the role →
  • Spanish translator So the fund and the directory are not English only. Project by projectRead the role →
  • Accessibility reviewer Test this site and its forms with the screen readers, switches, and magnifiers our athletes actually use, then tell us what breaks. A one time projectRead the role →
On the court

The part that looks like sport.

Hands on, in person, usually on a weekend.

  • Adaptive sports coach Run a clinic, coach a session, teach a sport. No adaptive experience required if you can coach. A season, or a single clinicRead the role →
  • Event crew Setup, registration, scorekeeping, teardown. The reason an event feels organized. A day at a timeRead the role →
  • Equipment technician Fit, repair, and refurbish chairs and adaptive gear. Rare, and worth more than most people realize. As it comes upRead the role →
  • Athlete mentor Be the person a newly injured athlete or their family calls first. Best done by someone who has been there. An hour here and thereRead the role →
  • Program scout Find and verify adaptive programs so Adaptive Sports Near Me is a real directory and not a list of guesses. An hour a monthRead the role →
  • Clinical referral partner Recreational therapists, physical therapists, and hospital staff who can hand someone a brochure the way one was handed to Alec. As it comes upRead the role →
No title required

You do not need a profession to change this.

Most people who want to help do not have a job title we listed. They have a relationship, a room, or a reason to organize something.

  • Make an introduction A company, a foundation, a venue, a coach, a reporter, a family who needs us. Warm introductions beat every cold ask we could write. One emailRead the role →
  • Host something A tournament, a clinic, a popcorn drive, a birthday fundraiser. We will hand you everything you need. A few weeks of planningRead the role →
  • Board and advisory The seats that steer this as it grows. We are deliberate about who sits in them, and we are looking. A real commitmentRead the role →
